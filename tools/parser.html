What happened

How it reads. Every tone counts, however short. In the first phrase the field forms as the max-min reading of its tones and sharpens as they arrive. When two mirror hearings tie, the ring shows the provisional one with the other as a dashed ghost; an arrival that crosses the two tones binding the tie settles it and relabels the phrase — a resolution, not a modulation. After that the field holds nothing but its orientation, set by the last phrase (a phrase ends at a pause). Each new phrase is heard in that orientation; if it places every tone, nothing changes, however it cadences. If it holds a tone that orientation cannot place, the phrase itself decides a new one — as little change as it must — and the field rotates for a one-semitone shift or morphs for anything larger. Non-diatonic colour is not handled yet. Tuning is read from the playing itself. The ring sits at waterline ρ = 1: the base circle equals the field's reach, so the deepest anti just touches the pole and every tone is admitted.

The mode light is the seat the music stands on, and the hierarchy that hangs from it — one structure. A root harmonizes with its up-stream chain neighbour (its 5th) first, then its 3rd, 7th, and on down the diatonic hierarchy: root, 5th, 3rd, 7th, 9th, 13th, 11th. A tonic/dominant pair — two diatonic tones five semitones apart anywhere in the phrase (diatonic in the orientation in force; anything outside it is passed over) — is the root and its 5th under exactly one root, the chain-forward member (the lower g‴: Ti +70 … Re 0 … Fa −70), and that root is the seat whichever way round the pair sounds (Do then Sol is still Do, a call; Do then Fa seats Fa, with Do its 5th). Each tone takes the most recent partner in the phrase; the seat is re-read on every tone and every turn of the field, and across the pause it holds. Resolution is hierarchical: a succession of two tones is read against the seat and resolves when it moves up the hierarchy toward the root. Ti→Do resolves in Do mode (7th to root); in Mi mode the same half step pulls the other way — Do→Ti resolves (13th to 5th) and Ti→Do is a call; in Sol mode Do is a suspension that releases to Ti (11th to 3rd). A resolution lands inside the seat's hierarchy, so it never moves the seat; only a pair does. With no seat nothing resolves — a bare line is contextualized by accompaniment the parser does not hear. The seat's letter is backlit on the ring and the trace is hazed in its colour; the readout names the degree each resolution lands on. Set the light to fixed to choose the seat yourself; that locks the ring to the orientation the seat implies, the trace paints in it, and the field never turns — bends and slides ride the curve like everything else. Off is off.

The trace is the input itself, before any decision: the fundamental contour, tracked continuously every 5 ms and never rounded to a grid, coloured by g(x), as thick as the tracker is confident. Faint dots are the pitches it considered and did not take; the faint spectrum underneath is the sound itself. Thin bars mark the tones handed to the decoder — that step (what counts as a tone, a glide, a phrase) is provisional. A piano with the pedal down is several voices at once, and a single contour cannot follow it reliably. Focus clears upper partials from the faint spectrum — a peak with a strong enough peak at a half, a third … an eighth of its frequency is taken for an overtone — so what remains is the fundamentals, all of them: several tones at once show as several lines. Bends and slides turn the field: a voice that moves continuously is heard as the field tone it left, displaced, and the field turns under it up to a semitone (π/6) and back as it is released; a voice that carries on to the next field tone arrives there as if it had stepped; a voice that starts on an anti and glides onto a field tone is that tone, approached. Nothing a bend passes through reaches the decoder as a tone. The field turns under the voice's centre — its mean position over the last quarter second — not its instant: a bend moves the centre and the field follows; a vibrato leaves the centre where it is, so the field holds and the voice rides the curve, the probe and the ribbon changing colour with it.
